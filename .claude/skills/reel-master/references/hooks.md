# Reel openings: the owner's guide (دليل بدايات الريلز — Time Keeper)

The owner's own guide, 7 Nov 2026, kept here as written in substance. It extends rule 6f in `storytelling.md` and
wins over any other hook advice in this skill. Check every script against it before the readers see it.

**The goal:** in the first 3 seconds the viewer stops, wonders, and wants the story.
**The base rule:** never open by explaining the topic. Open on something that makes the viewer ask «ليش؟» or
«شلون؟». Not «اليوم بنتكلم عن تاريخ دينيسون», but «ليش إذا فتحت رولكس قديمة… تلقى اسم شركة ثانية داخلها؟»: a
question the film answers only inside the story.

## 1. The five kinds of opening

| Kind | Example | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Mystery: why is this here? | «ليش إذا فتحت رولكس قديمة… تلقى اسم شركة ثانية داخلها؟» | history, hidden details |
| The strange thing: an unexpected shot | «ليش حاطّين ساعة برّا الغواصة؟» | strange events, watch trials |
| The visual question: the picture before the words | «ليش ساعة حاطّينها بحوض ماي… وحولها سمچ؟» | a strong, clear picture from frame 1 |
| Surprise: a fact against expectation | «ساعة ما فيها عقارب… شلون تعرف الوقت؟» | unusual designs and movements |
| The problem: what happened, what solved it | «صنع الساعة… بس اكتشف إن محد يقدر يصلّحها!» | inventions and mistakes, only when documented |

## 2. Fixed rules for every opening

| Rule | Required |
|---|---|
| Length | 5–12 words where possible |
| Duration | 2–4 s |
| Dialect | natural Kuwaiti, not fusha |
| First shot | shows the thing we are talking about |
| Curiosity | one clear question, not several |
| Information | do not reveal the answer in the opening |
| Credibility | never invent a story to grab attention (title and caption included) |

An opening need not be a question: sometimes a strange sentence is stronger.

## 3. The shape after the opening (a guide, not a law)

| Time | Part | Example (Rolex ep3) |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | HOOK: something that raises curiosity | «ليش حاطّين ساعة برّا الغواصة؟» |
| 3–15 s | SETUP: the start of the story | who built the sub, and why it was special |
| 15–50 s | STORY: the event and the challenge | Rolex joins the trial, the watch goes down |
| 50–75 s | PAYOFF: the result | the sub comes back… and the watch still runs |
| last 5–10 s | ENDING: what the story means | «رولكس ما اكتفت تقول إن ساعاتها تتحمّل… خلّت العالم يشوف الدليل.» |

What matters most: every line pushes the story forward.

## 4. Hook checklist before production (all five, or rewrite)

1. Does the viewer understand the question without explanation?
2. Does the first shot clearly show the strange thing?
3. Does the opening open real curiosity?
4. Is the answer inside the film?
5. Does it sound natural in Kuwaiti?

## 5. Fixed instructions for the script writer

1. Pull out the strangest or strongest **documented** fact in the story.
2. Write **5 different openings**, short and Kuwaiti.
3. Pick the one that opens the strongest curiosity and can be shown from the first frame.
4. Do not open with the brand name or the year unless the story needs it.
5. Do not open with «تدرون», «اليوم بنتكلم» or «خلوني أقول لكم».
6. No exaggeration, no question with an obvious answer, no misleading fact.
7. Do not answer the question straight away; let the events reveal it.
8. Tie the opening to the ending so the viewer feels the story is complete.
9. Do not turn the film into a spec sheet; specs appear when the story needs them.
10. When the story is about a product we sell, the opening is not an ad; the sale comes naturally at the end.

The aim: a true, enjoyable story that holds the viewer to the end, not a history lesson and not a plain ad.
**Measure the hook by retention in the first seconds** (`insights.md`), not by views alone. Try different openings
and see what keeps people watching, instead of assuming one kind of question always works.
