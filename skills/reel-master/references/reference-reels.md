## Reel 2: the tree of troubles (65.5 s)
A town hangs its troubles in cloth bundles on one tree; each person may take someone else's bundle, opens it, and
takes their own back. Then a modern young man on his sofa, scrolling holiday photos; the lesson; the tree again.
- **The hook is a strange picture, described:** «Hundreds of cloth bundles hang from the branches of a giant tree as
  an entire town stands underneath, staring up.» The first shot is a close-up of one bundle being tied.
- **Threes, twice:** three envies (the baker, the widow, the injured man), then three reveals (the gambling son, the
  childless couple, the blind farmer), each on its own 1–2 s shot. The turn comes as a line about faces: «their
  faces change».
- **Faster than Reel 1** (45 shots a minute), and every exchange is a close-up: the emotion carried by the face and
  the hands (eyes, a tear, glasses taken off). This is storytelling rule 18p, at work.
- **Light by act:** warm afternoon for the town; a dark living room lit by a lamp and a phone for the modern scene;
  bright saturated colour for the «polished outsides» (the beach, the house, the family); golden light for the close.
- **One quotable line** at the turn: «we compare our messy insides to everyone else's polished outsides».
- **The close:** the young man in the town, holding his own bundle, and «Follow or you might miss the next lesson».
- Confirms ai-film.md: the music sits close under the voice (6.8 dB), there are no silences, and the cuts are hard.

## Reel 3: the quantum corral, «Quantum Physics from Scratch, lecture five» (75.5 s)
Scientists place 48 iron atoms in a ring on copper, and ripples appear inside it; the film explains why, step by step.
- **The hook in three beats (0–19 s):** the striking real image (the ring and its ripples), the question it raises
  («If every iron atom is on the edge, where did those ripples come from?»), and a promise: «By the end of this video
  you'll understand…». Only then the title card: the series name, «Lecture Five», the episode title.
- **A series:** every frame carries the series name and the lecture number at the top, and the last line asks for
  the next one: «Follow for lecture six». The follow has a reason.
- **One idea per screen, built in front of you:** a section title in big type («Electrons on copper», «Where waves
  overlap», «What stays still?», «Why rings?», «What the tip detects»), one diagram under it that draws itself as the
  voice speaks (a wave, then a second wave, then their sum), and a 2–6 word caption under it. Few cuts; the motion
  is the diagram.
- **The close returns to the opening image,** now explained («The ripples explained»).
- **Slower and quieter than the parables:** 2.86 words/s, pauses up to 0.9 s, the music 16 dB under: a teacher, not
  a storyteller.
- It labels every picture as «reconstruction», «simulated scan» or «schematic». **Not for us:** the owner has ruled
  out «رسم توضيحي مبسّط» labels (storytelling 18f); our honesty goes in the credits and the caption.

## Reel 4: a competitor's Rolex story, «Rolex were marketing geniuses» (94 s)
Sent by the owner on 4 Nov 2026: «this is our competitor… almost we match in ideas, but we are in different style and
different length». It covers in one English Reel what «حكاية رولكس» spreads over episode 1 and «السباحة», then three

- **The numbers:** 94 s; 50 shots, **31.9 a minute**, 1.9 s on average (1.0–4.2); 267 words, **2.87 words/s**, longest
  pause 0.94 s; the music 11 dB under the voice; every sentence within -14.7 to -18.0 dB.
- **The look:** flat mid-century editorial illustration (ochre, navy, cream), one palette for the whole film; most
  shots move a little, a few hold. Captions are 1–3 words, condensed capitals, with one gold word.
- **The hook is a claim with a promise:** «Rolex were marketing geniuses, and here's why». The viewer stays to see it
  proven; every story after it is one piece of evidence.
- **The close answers the hook in one line:** «Wilsdorf sold a tool and let the people who used it write the ads».
- **Each phrase gets its own literal picture:** «in his pocket» shows a pocket watch, «on the wrist» shows a bracelet,
  «like a jam jar» shows the screw-down crown. The cut lands on the phrase.
- **Concrete, physical details carry the facts:** «the size of a coin», «the top deck of a horse-drawn bus», «a jam
  jar», «51-degree water», «seven miles down».
- **Its facts need care:** «got Hillary to the top» implies a Rolex on Hillary, whose summit watch is disputed (a
  Smiths by his own account; Tenzing wore a Rolex). The fish bowl window has no firm year (a 1920s–1930s campaign).

## What Time Keeper takes
1. **«How it works» as a numbered series** (script type `how_it_works`): the series name and the lesson number on
   every frame (for example «الساعات من الصفر · الدرس 3»), the hook in three beats (the real watch or movement
   doing the strange thing, the question, «بنهاية الفيديو بتعرف…»), a title card after the hook, one idea per
   screen with its own big title and a diagram that builds while the voice speaks (the engine's `technical`,
   `blueprint` and `data` looks do this for free), the close back on the first image, explained, then the brand
   (rule 6e), and a last line that opens the next lesson (never a spoken «تابعنا»). Each lesson is one complication, named (storytelling 8b): the first proposed is the
   perpetual calendar (تقويم دائم), how a watch knows February has 28 or 29 days. Pace: about 2.9 words a second, pauses up to 0.9 s, the music felt, not heard.
2. **Parables and stories (the AI-film route)** keep Reel 1's numbers, and may go as fast as Reel 2 (35–45 shots a
   minute): threes for the examples, a close-up for every reaction, one quotable line at the turn, light by act.
3. **Every film:** the first and last shots rhyme. **No spoken follow request** (the owner, 3 Nov 2026, on «حكاية
   رولكس» episode 1: «remove this: تابعنا للحلقة الثانية»): the cliffhanger line («والحل… لقاه بالبحر.») and the series
   name on screen carry the next episode; the voice never asks to follow.
4. **From the competitor's Rolex Reel (Reel 4):** open a series episode on a claim that the episode proves, and close
   on one line that answers it; give each phrase its own picture and the cut its phrase; carry the facts on
   physical comparisons the viewer can see. Take its stories (the fish bowl window, Everest 1953, Trieste 1960) as
   leads for our episodes, checked against our own sources and told in our words, never its script. Keep our own
   look, Kuwaiti voice and one story per episode: the owner compares the numbers before deciding on length.
