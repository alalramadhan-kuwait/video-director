# The reader panel: three readers, a filter, a vote, and Étienne (rule 6d)

The owner: «Always after writing each script, assign three agents, one with zero knowledge of the watch world, another
with medium knowledge, and the last one an expert, and make them read that text. Make sure the story makes sense …
those agents raise their concerns, one guy filters them and checks whichever apply, and all three vote that the script
makes sense, a chain of events that does not lose the listener or raise questions.»

The example that started it: «وفي الحادية عشرة اشترى أول ساعة برج» — a reader who does not know the watch world does
not know what a tower clock is, nor why a boy would buy one, nor what it says about him. Every fact must carry its
meaning for the listener, or go.

## When
After every script and every rewrite of one, **before** the owner sees it, and before any voice is recorded.

The owner named them («عطى كل قاراءة من الثلاث اسم»): **Noura**, **Fahad** and **Bu Khaled**. Use their names in the
agents' prompts and when telling the owner what each one said.

## 1. Three readers, in parallel (fresh agents, background)
Write the script to `panel/<film>/script_vN.md`: per line what is **said**, what is **written** on screen and what is
**seen** — no sources, no production notes (the readers judge what the viewer gets). Each reader reads only that file
and uses no other tool: their knowledge is the persona.

| Reader | Persona | Watches for |
|---|---|---|
| **Noura** (نورة), the novice | knows nothing of the watch world; a smart Kuwaiti viewer scrolling Instagram; **loves simple sentences**: short, one idea each, plain words, nothing she has to read twice | words and names she cannot place, a missing «why», jumps, open questions, where she would swipe; and every long or tangled sentence, which she rewrites as short simple ones |
| **Fahad** (فهد), medium | owns a few good watches, knows Rolex/Patek/AP, auctions, roughly what a complication is; not a collector of independents | the same, plus being talked down to (seconds spent explaining what they know) |
| **Bu Khaled** (بو خالد), the expert | a collector who knows the subject deeply and comments publicly on mistakes | wrong or misleading facts, a picture that would not match, the thing that really matters left out, nothing new for a collector |

The novice also flags **every word they would not say themselves** and every figure of speech they have to decode
(«يصنع في الظلّ», «جفّت الطلبات», «العتيقة», «دور»), even when they could guess it from the rest of the line: the
owner caught these after the panel had passed them (rule 9c).

Noura also **simplifies** (the owner: «وخل نورة تحب تبسط الجمل بدون تعقيد تكون لغة بسيطة»): for every sentence
with more than one idea, a chain of commas, a clause inside a clause, or more than about 15 words, she writes the
simple version — two or three short sentences in plain Standard Arabic — and the filter takes it unless it loses a
fact or a second it cannot spare.

Each reports, per point: line, the exact words, the question in the viewer's own voice, severity (SWIPE / CONFUSED /
MINOR, and FACT for the expert), a short fix that replaces words rather than adding lines; then the second they would
most likely swipe and whether the story reads as one chain of events. Under 450 words each.

## 2. The filter (one fresh agent)
Gets the script, the three reports and the research notes. For every point it decides **apply** or **drop** with a
one-line reason: apply what any reader would trip on (a novice's «what is that?» counts when a fact's meaning is
missing, not when a name is merely unfamiliar but explained by the picture); check every FACT point against the notes (and the source pages when the notes are unclear)
(a fact the notes do not support is cut or softened, never kept on a reader's say-so); drop what costs more seconds
than it earns (the Reel stays 60–90 s, rule 20b). It writes the revised lines and the reason for each change.

## 3. The vote
Apply the filter's changes (`script_vN+1.md`), then send the new script back to the same three readers (continue them,
so they remember their concerns). Each votes **yes** (makes sense, one chain of events, nothing left open that would
lose me) or **no** with the line that still fails. Three yes → the script goes to the owner, with the panel's notes in
a short line. A no → back to the filter. At most three rounds; after the third, send the script with the open points
named.

## 4. Étienne: the brand, the design and the Reel (the owner's fifth member)
The owner: «I do want to add the one who holds the brand identity and design and tells you what is okay and not okay,
and who's expert in the media and social media and the hooks and attraction and views retaining … for the reel to go
viral … a French guy who is very neat and detailed.»

**Étienne** (إتيان) — a Parisian, once creative director for the social accounts of luxury houses; precise to the
pixel and the half-second, polite and unbending. He reads with:
- `brand/timekeeper.json` and `profiles/timekeeper-luxury-doc.yaml` (colours, type, logo, the composed luxury tone,
  people never cartoonish, watches never drawn), `vendor/paper-story/references/hooks.md` (the hook library and the
  log of what each film's numbers taught), and storytelling rules 6, 6c, 18–20c.

He sits in at two moments:
1. **The script** (with Noura, Fahad and Bu Khaled, and votes with them): the hook — is it the strongest true moment,
   said in the first two seconds, with the subject in frame 1; what keeps the viewer at 3, 10 and 30 seconds; the
   length against the platform (60–90 s on Reels); the ending that makes people follow, save or share; and whether
   the words and the plan fit the brand.
2. **The finished film** (beside the critic, from the same round folder): every first frame and the cover, the brand
   colours and type, alignment and margins, text a phone can read, the pace (a visible change every 2–3 s, no long
   still holds), the music, the end card, and the post itself (caption, first line, hashtags, the best hour to post
   for a Kuwaiti audience). His **brand no** is a blocker: the film does not go to the owner with it.

When the owner sends a film's numbers, Étienne reads them first and writes the lesson (rule 6c).
His report: what is wrong, where (second or line), why it costs views or the brand, and the exact fix.
